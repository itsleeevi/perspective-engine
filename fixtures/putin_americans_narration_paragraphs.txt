The most powerful country on earth claps when the plane lands.

I have the picture. In a folder. On my desk. That is page one on Americans. It is funny. The next pages are not a joke. Stay.

I do not hate you. Hate is for people who keep you up at night. I sleep fine. You are a Tuesday. Weather with a credit card.

You know my name because it is easy to yell. I know your type because it is easy to guess. I have been waiting you out since flip phones.

The first American I ever met was not a person. It was a commercial. A sandwich the size of a steering wheel. White teeth. A red cup you could hide a dog in.

A fake family hugging in a fake kitchen. They paid people to hug. You watched it and got homesick for a house you do not have. That is not TV. That is you, with a song.

You talk in restaurants like nobody else is eating. You ask the waiter what he wants to do with his life. He wants you to order. You wear the brand on purpose. You paid to be a walking ad.

You clap when the plane lands. I already said it. The pilot did his job. You still clap. In school they draw you with a flag on the sleeve and no idea where anything is. That drawing is not an insult. It is instructions.

I write down the smile. You smile at cameras. At cashiers. At the guy who just overcharged you. A smile is a tell. You want to be liked more than you want to win. Winning does not get a like.

I watch your movies like a butcher watches a cow. The hero breaks the rules and gets a speech. The fight ends because the music gets loud. I do not believe the movies. I believe that you do.

By the time you walk into a real room, a cartoon of you is already sitting there, eating chips. You argue with the cartoon for twenty minutes. The cartoon is more consistent than you are. You call that personality.

Second folder. Thinner. Cost more. Not what you say. You say everything. It is what you buy when you think nobody is keeping score.

You will sell a principle for a cheaper flight. You will sell a friend for a better deal, then post a sunset about loyalty. You will sell a secret to feel like the smartest guy at the table.

Money is easy. Money shows up on paper. Ego does not. I lead with a badge. A backstage pass. Someone important who remembered your name, or pretended to.

You would trade a nuclear secret for a good seat. I am only half joking. The half that is joking is still taking notes.

You think you are a citizen. I think you are a market with fireworks. Markets crash. Then they come back. Same product. New color.

Here is the sentence. The one I promised. You come back. You come back thirsty. You come back with a coupon. That is the most useful line in the whole folder. Screenshot it. I already knew you would.

Now the number. Your toys are real. Ships. Satellites. A budget with too many zeros. I will say that in private. I am not lying. That should worry you more.

I do not respect the mood. Hardware is a receipt. Will is a mood. I counted how long you argue with yourselves before anything gets done.

The number is this. Shorter than your attention span. Longer than your protests. You get loud. Then you get bored. Then you go home. A long hard job does not trend. A football game does.

I do not need you weak. Weak is messy. I need you busy. Three crises. A midterm. A football game. That is a superpower on mute.

The soldier in my folder is not a coward. He goes home when the pictures get bad and the comments get mean. I plan around the pictures. I plan around the fact that your news still sells that red cup. Yes. That cup again. You remember it. That is the point.

This is the page that should make you sit up. You change the plan of a nuclear country the way other people change a kettle. On a Tuesday. With a parade. With a song you already knew.

A deal is not a deal. It is a mood that dies in January, next to the gym membership. I do not take your word. I take your calendar.

How many months until your election. How many months until the circus with different hats. Then you picked the loud guy. The hair. The rallies. The phone at three in the morning.

Then you picked the other guy. Then you picked the loud guy again. I did not even change chairs. You think I pick your president. Presidents are a costume. I pick the fight you are in.

August to November is a holiday I did not pay for. You think consistency is a good thing. With you, it is a problem I do not have. I do not talk you into anything. I wait until you talk yourselves into the opposite, on the same stage, and call it a win. I call it a sequel you paid to watch twice.

Same folder. New name on the tab. I have used the same pencil for three of your presidents. It is not even a nice pencil.

I do not invent your fights. You publish them. You fund them. You put a donate button under them. A panel about America falling apart, with sandwiches. A famous person reading a speech about a country they could not find on a map an hour ago. The lighting is great.

The choir is not secret. It has a tote bag and a podcast. We used to plant stories. Now we hit like. Find the American already screaming at America. Give him a bigger room. He will do the rest himself.

You call it a marketplace of ideas. I call it a buffet. I do not cook. I show up hungry, while you fight about the spoons. The most valuable American is not a spy. Spies want money. This one wants to be right on the internet, for free, and calls it being real.

If you are waiting for a villain in a dark coat with an envelope, you have the wrong century. The envelope is a quote. The quote is yours. I did not even edit it.

Now the other American. The one I actually drink with. Not the cartoon. The cartoon is TV, and TV is for you. These ones are in London, on a boat with no flag. Quiet is expensive.

They talk about democracy at lunch. At dinner they ask if the sanction list has a hole with a last name. The last name is never theirs, out loud. You are two countries with one language. One votes. One sends the invoice. Guess which number I keep.

I watched a senator's kid at a table his dad could not be photographed at. I did not gasp. Gasping is your hobby. I checked the time.

Your politics have a cleaner word. Machine. Machines jam. They make the same noise before they fail. You film the noise and argue about who made it. Fifty percent plus one. That is the religion. A coin flip with fireworks.

You think I want you divided. That is cute. Division is the exhaust. The engine is your attention, pointed at a clip, at a guy in a supermarket who said the wrong three words into a phone.

A country staring at itself cannot stare out the window. Every time your news eats a week, the rest of the world does not wait. You find out later. On a documentary. Sad piano. Three years later. Eating the sandwich from the commercial. Yes. That sandwich. I told you I would bring it back.

The machine is not evil. Evil is a movie word. You like movie words. The machine is predictable. That is the nicest thing I say about an enemy. Congratulations. You earned it.

A new American walks on stage and talks about a reset. A line he will not cross this time. He swears. On his kids' brand deals. I do not cheer. Panic is cardio. I open the same folder. The cartoon is still in it. The coupon. The soldier who goes home when the comments get mean. The loud guy. The other guy. The loud guy again.

The headings have not moved in thirty years. Only the photos. You age. The file does not. That should offend you more than I do. I do not need you worse. I need you you. Loud. Rich. Impatient. Sure that history is a sequel and you still have the best explosions.

You will ask if I am right. Of course. You love a winner. Tomorrow you will fight in the comments. Genius or cartoon. I predicted that too. Last page. Under the coupon.

Someone is writing a speech about how this time is different. That sentence is older than your last three slogans. In a cold room, I sharpen a pencil. It is not dramatic. It is maintenance.

This does not keep going because I am a genius. Genius is a compliment you give so you do not have to change. It keeps going because you are familiar. And familiarity, my American friends, is the most expensive thing you give away for free.

Send this to someone who clapped when the plane landed. They will not thank you. I will.
